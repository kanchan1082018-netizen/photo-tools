How to Print 300 DPI Passport Photos on A4 & 4×6 Paper Without Scaling Errors (Complete Studio Calibration Guide)

Even when you crop a passport portrait to exact biometric dimensions on screen, a single misconfigured checkbox in your Windows, macOS, or mobile print dialog can shrink a 35×45 mm photo down to 32×41 mm—rendering the entire sheet unusable at embassies or passport offices. Furthermore, rigid CSS grid templates leave up to 40% of expensive glossy paper empty whenever you mix portrait sizes with ID cards. This engineering guide explains exact 300 DPI pixel math, printer driver overrides, and zero-waste sheet packing.

2. Step-by-Step: Mandatory Printer Settings (Epson, Canon, HP & Brother)

Follow these exact five steps whenever printing from Chrome, Edge, Windows Photo Viewer, or macOS Preview:

  1. Step 1 — Match Physical & Digital Paper Sizes: Ensure the paper size chosen under QuickPhotoGrid's Paper Layout panel (e.g., A4 or 4×6) matches the exact paper size selected inside your printer driver's Printing Preferences.
  2. Step 2 — Disable "Fit to Page" / Enable "100% Scale": Expand More Settings in your browser print dialog and change Scale from Default / Fit to Printable Area to Custom: 100 (or Actual Size).
  3. Step 3 — Set Margins to None (or Use Outer Sheet Margin Slider): Set browser margins to None. QuickPhotoGrid's built-in Outer Sheet Margin slider (default 11 mm on A4 and 4 mm on 4×6) already reserves safe grip space for your printer's feed rollers.
  4. Step 4 — Select Photo Paper Media Type: In your Epson (L805/L3210), Canon (G3010/Pixma), or HP Smart Tank driver, switch Media Type from Plain Paper to Photo Paper Glossy or Matte Photo Paper so the printer uses high-density pigment/dye ink droplets without bleeding.
  5. Step 5 — Verify with a Millimeter Ruler: After printing, measure one photo edge with a standard ruler—it will match 35.0 × 45.0 mm (or 51.0 × 51.0 mm) to sub-millimeter accuracy.

3. Quick Troubleshooting: Fixing Common Print Issues

  • Edges Cut Off on 4×6 Borderless Prints: Borderless printer drivers naturally expand the image by ~2% ("Expansion / Amount of Extension") to prevent white borders. Set Expansion to Min in your printer driver or increase QuickPhotoGrid's Outer Sheet Margin to 5 mm.
  • Horizontal Lines or Banding on Glossy Paper: Run a quick Print Head Nozzle Check in your printer utility and switch Print Quality from Standard to High / Best.
  • Colors Look Washed Out or Too Dark: Ensure you loaded the coated glossy side of the photo paper facing the print head, and use QuickPhotoGrid's Brightness & Contrast sliders (+5% brightness) if your specific ink set prints slightly warm.

4. Frequently Asked Questions (300 DPI Sheet Printing)

Should I export as PNG or JPEG for retail photo lab kiosks (CVS, Walgreens, Local Labs)?

Both Download PNG and Download JPEG inside QuickPhotoGrid export at full 300 DPI resolution. JPEG is universally accepted by instant photo kiosks and USB print machines, while PNG offers lossless sharpness for desktop inkjet printing.

How do Dashed Cutting Lines help when trimming 35 photos on an A4 sheet?

Enabling Dashed Cutting Lines draws ultra-fine 1 px alignment guides around each photo footprint, allowing you to slice entire rows cleanly with a paper trimmer or scissors without leaving uneven white borders.
